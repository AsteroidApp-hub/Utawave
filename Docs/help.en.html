01Getting Started & The Interface

Utawave is a DAW built specifically for recording vocal covers. It cuts out as much complex setup as possible, so you can get through the core flow as quickly as possible: load a backing track, record, and export.

Layout of the Screen

  • Top (Toolbar): Play / Stop / Record buttons, the playback time display, tempo (BPM), and toggles for the metronome and looping.
  • Left (TRACKS panel): The list of tracks. For each track you get volume (Vol), left/right balance (Pan), reverb (Rev), M Mute, S Solo, R record arm, I input monitor, and more.
  • Center (Timeline): Your recorded vocals and imported tracks appear here as "waveforms" laid out left to right. This is where you cut and move things around.
  • Right (MASTER panel): The final volume and meters for the whole song. It can be collapsed.
Tip: Most actions can be done with either a button or the keyboard. Start with the buttons, and once you're comfortable, switch to shortcuts to work faster.

02Project Basics

Your work is saved as a "project" (the file extension is .uta). Think of it as one project per song.

New, Open, and Save

  • New: "File" → "New Project..."
  • Open: "File" → "Open..." (Ctrl+O). You can also open from "Recent Projects" on the startup screen.
  • Save: Ctrl+S. Save As is Ctrl+Shift+S.

Auto-Save and Backups

Your work is saved automatically at regular intervals, and timestamped generational backups are kept as well. Even if the app closes unexpectedly, you'll be asked whether to recover the next time you open the same project.

Audio files live inside the project: Imported and recorded audio is copied into the Audio folder within the project folder. When handing a project off to someone else, share the entire folder.

03Importing a Backing Track

Start by loading your accompaniment (off-vocal / instrumental track).

  1. Click the IMPORT button at the top right, or choose "File" → "Import Audio..." (Ctrl+I). You can also drag and drop an audio file onto the timeline. The IMPORT button accepts both audio and MIDI, automatically detecting which by the file extension.
  2. The imported track is automatically placed at the start of the song (0 seconds).

Supported formats: WAV / AIFF / MP3 / FLAC / Ogg and other common formats (on macOS, m4a / aac and more are also supported; the exact set varies slightly by OS). Files with a different sample rate are converted automatically on import.

Volume is matched automatically: On import, the volume (loudness) is automatically adjusted to a target level (you can turn this off in the settings).

04Setting Up to Record

  1. Add a track: Use the + button at the bottom left of the TRACKS panel (or "+ Add Track" along the bottom) to choose mono or stereo. Vocal recording is usually fine in mono.
  2. Choose the input: In the track's "In:" menu, select the mic (input channel) you'll use.
  3. Record arm: Light up the R button on the track you want to record to. This puts it in the "record to this track" state.
  4. Check your voice: Turn on I (input monitor) to hear yourself back and watch the meter move. Sing and confirm the meter responds.
Recording multiple tracks at once: Light up R on several tracks and assign each a different input, and you can record to multiple tracks simultaneously.
If you get no sound or no input: Open "File" → "Preferences" and, in the audio settings, make sure your audio device (audio interface) is selected.

05Tempo & Metronome

  • Tempo (BPM): Double-click the BPM display in the toolbar to type a value directly.
  • Set it by tapping: Tap the TAP button along with the song to estimate the tempo.
  • Detect it automatically: Right-click an imported track and choose "Detect Tempo" to automatically estimate the BPM from the accompaniment.
  • Metronome: Use the CLICK button to turn the click sound on / off. You can also adjust its volume and more on the dedicated click track.
  • Count-in: You can set how many bars of count-in play before recording starts (in Preferences).

06Recording

  1. Confirm that R is lit on the track you want to record to.
  2. Move the playback position to where you want recording to start (click the ruler, or move bar by bar with N / B).
  3. Press the R key (or the toolbar's record button) to start recording. If a count-in is set, it plays first before recording begins.
  4. Press R again, or Space, to stop.

Handy Ways to Record

  • Punch-in: While playing back, press R at the spot you want to redo, and only that part is overwritten (the seams are smoothed automatically).
  • Loop recording: Press L to play a loop range over and over while recording take after take.
  • Capture retroactively: While playing back, your singing is always being captured in the background. If you think "that one was good," press Ctrl+Shift+R to retroactively commit the recording.

07Managing Takes

When you re-record over the same spot, the previous take isn't deleted; it's automatically moved aside to a take lane. You can compare them and pick the best one.

  • Use the track's TList button to show / hide the take lanes.
  • Use S (Solo) on a take to listen to just that take.
  • Use the ↑ button on the take you want (or Shift+↑) to promote that take to the main lane.

08Editing Waveforms

Each chunk of audio on the timeline is called a "waveform." You can edit them intuitively with the mouse.

Fades and Crossfades

  • Fade in / out: Select a range and press F. This smooths the start and end.
  • Crossfade: Select the overlapping or touching part of two waveforms and press X. This prevents the clicking noise at the seam (you can also right-click → "Draw Crossfade").

Adjusting Volume

  • Per-waveform volume: Use the waveform's fader to raise or lower it. To vary the volume within a single waveform, place points on the volume line (breakpoints) for fine control.
  • Cut silence: Right-click → "Cut Silence" to automatically remove quiet parts such as breaths.
Zooming in and out: Use Ctrl+mouse wheel to zoom horizontally, and Shift+F to fit the whole song on screen.

09Key Change & Tempo Detection

  • Change the key (pitch): Right-click a waveform → "Change Key..." to choose from -6 to +6 semitones. High-quality processing transposes it while preserving sound quality. The result is saved as a new file, so with the same setting it applies instantly next time. Choose "0 (original pitch)" to return to the original.
  • Find the tempo: Right-click a waveform → "Detect Tempo" to automatically estimate the accompaniment's BPM.

10Effects (FX)

You can add plugins such as reverb and compressors to individual tracks or to the whole mix (master).

Opening the FX Slots

Press the FX button at the bottom left of the TRACKS panel to show the effect slots (INS) on the right side of each track. Press it again to hide them.

  • Click an empty slot → choose the plugin you want to add it.
  • Click the chip for an added plugin to open its settings window.
  • Ctrl+click to temporarily turn it off (bypass). Drag to reorder it or move it to another track (Alt+drag to copy).
If your plugins don't show up: Open "File" → "Manage Plugins..." and run a scan. Your installed plugins will appear in the list.

Adding Reverb

Use each track's Rev slider to add reverb to that track. You can also apply it only to the live monitor ("foldback") while recording, and even then it is not written into the recorded file.

11Importing & Entering MIDI

You can import MIDI data (.mid files) to use as a guide melody, harmony, or accompaniment. You can also enter notes by hand.

Importing a MIDI File

  1. Choose "File" → "Import MIDI..." and open a .mid file (you can also drag and drop it onto the timeline).
  2. In the "Import MIDI" dialog, check the tracks you want to import (each track's channel and note count are shown).
  3. Choose the placement: "Place at start" or "Place at current playback position." If needed, check "Import tempo & time signature" and "Import markers."
  4. Press "Import" and the selected tracks are added as MIDI tracks.
To make them sound: Imported MIDI tracks play with a built-in tone. Insert a sound-source plugin of your choice to play them with that tone (add it the same way as in Effects (FX)).

Entering Notes by Hand (Creating Harmonies & Guides)

  • From the add-track menu, choose "Add MIDI Track."
  • Double-click the timeline to create a region for note entry, then double-click that region to open the piano roll (the keyboard editing view).
  • In the piano roll, double-click an empty area to add a note, drag to move or resize, and press Delete to delete. Use ↑ / ↓ to move up or down a semitone.

12Exporting (WAV / MP3)

When you're done, export to an audio file.

  1. Open the EXPORT button at the top right, or "File" → "Export..." (Ctrl+E).
  2. Choose the format (WAV / AIFF / MP3) and the range (whole song / selected range / bar range).
  3. Choose whether to render the whole mix to one file or split it per track (stems), then export.
Clipping protection: On export, if the audio is too loud, it's automatically turned down a little to prevent clipping (you can turn this off in the settings).

15Troubleshooting

"Audio file not found" appears

The audio the project references may have been moved or deleted. Put the original file back into the project's Audio folder and it will be restored automatically.

My last session is gone / the app quit

When you reopen a saved project, if a newer backup exists you'll be asked whether to recover. Choosing "Recover" restores the most recent state.

My mic isn't recording

  • Check that Utawave is allowed to use the mic in your OS's microphone permissions.
  • In the audio settings under Preferences, check that the correct input device and channel are selected.
  • Check that the track's R (record arm) is lit.

I hear my voice with a delay (monitoring latency)

On Windows with an audio interface, selecting ASIO (the driver bundled with your interface) as the driver type in the audio settings improves this dramatically. Lowering the buffer size also reduces the delay (if you go too small and the sound drops out, step it back up one notch).

My recorded vocal sounds behind the backing track

Utawave automatically compensates for your device's latency when recording. If it still drifts, gradually increase the Additional Recording Offset (ms) under "Recording Flow" in Preferences (a positive value moves it earlier).

A plugin isn't in the list

Open "File" → "Manage Plugins..." and run "Rescan All." Plugins installed just now will be recognized too. Plugins that misbehave are automatically excluded during the scan (the app won't crash).
